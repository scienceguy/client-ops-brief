Client Ops Brief #2: Stand out, get paid, redesign the model

Consultants are drowning in their own output. This week covers the AI sameness trap, a veteran's case for killing the billable hour, one operator's struggle to turn effort into conversations, and how cross-border payment rails quietly eat margin. The common thread: direction beats volume.

This week

AI is making consultants forgettable

What happened. Most consulting business owners are falling into an AI trap, according to Consulting Success. Because AI draws from the average of everything, the plans, proposals, and marketing it generates are the same ones it hands the next person asking a similar question. More output does not equal more growth: one consulting business grew 56% year over year, and AI was nowhere near the reason.

Why it matters. For a solo operator the danger is invisible: you feel productive while your positioning blends into the crowd. The edge that remains is lived experience and judgment, which AI structurally cannot offer.

Do this. Audit one proposal or piece of marketing you shipped this month and ask whether a competitor could have generated the same thing in 30 seconds. If yes, add one client-specific detail only you know.

The billable hour is on its way out

What happened. In Consulting Success podcast episode 386, Will Hinde, President of Consulting Services at Addison Group with over 25 years in consulting and $900M+ in career revenue, argues we may be heading toward the death of the traditional billable hour. He points to productization, intellectual property, and co-sharing risk on implementation as the future, and says smaller firms may have an advantage because they lack the baggage of large offshore delivery models. He also stresses specialization: 'the riches are in the niches.'

Why it matters. For a one to five person firm, value is shifting from hours to owned IP and repeatable delivery. A niche you own beats a broad menu you rent.

Do this. List your three most profitable project types and pick one to turn into a fixed-scope, fixed-price offer this quarter.

Working harder is not the same as selling

What happened. A 40-year-old consultant with a 10-plus year background in business transformation posted on r/consulting that they have been building an advisory business since 2023. They developed an offering, built a website, published a book, created content, approached potential partners, and worked on distribution, but are struggling to turn that effort into meaningful client conversations or revenue, and are fed up with running and following up.

Why it matters. This is the trap many solo operators fall into: shipping assets instead of conversations. Distribution without a follow-up system produces visibility, not pipeline.

Do this. Replace one content task this week with five direct follow-ups to people who already know your work. Track replies, not impressions.

Getting paid across borders costs more than you think

What happened. WorldFirst published a comparison of freelance payment methods for African freelancers. The main options are bank transfers via SWIFT, which usually take one to five business days; freelance marketplaces, which offer payment protection and dispute processes but charge commissions that reduce net earnings; payment gateways, which settle in minutes to several days but carry transaction fees, currency conversion costs, and chargeback risk; and multi-currency accounts that let freelancers hold earnings and convert on their own terms.

Why it matters. Even if your clients are local, the principle holds: every payment rail has a fee, a delay, and a failure mode. Knowing your all-in cost per invoice protects delivery time from admin churn.

Do this. Calculate the all-in cost of your last three invoices including fees and conversion. If it is over 3%, price your next proposal to absorb it or switch rails.

Tool of the week

Loom

Async video updates replace status meetings and keep delivery time protected. A three-minute recording answers what a 30-minute call used to.

Use it: Record one weekly client update as a Loom instead of scheduling a status call this week.

The pattern

The through-line this week: activity is cheap and judgment is scarce. Consultants who ship more of the same get buried, while those who pick a niche, own their IP, and run clean payment ops keep their time for work that pays. Protect delivery time by cutting anything that does not move a specific client conversation forward.

One thing to try

Open the last proposal you sent and add one client-specific detail that no competitor could generate from a template. That is the smallest test of whether your positioning is yours.
